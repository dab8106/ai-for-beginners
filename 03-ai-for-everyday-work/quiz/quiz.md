# Quiz: AI for everyday work

10 questions. Try each one before you look at the answer: the correct option is in **bold**, and the short explanation under it says why. Aim for 7 out of 10. If you miss one, rewatch the lecture named next to it.

**1. You need a useful summary of a 60-page report before a meeting. What's the best prompt?** _([Lecture 13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=blIzU7NFEMg))_

- Summarise this
- Make it shorter
- **I'm the sales head. Summarise this in five bullet points on risks, costs and deadlines for my region, with a page number for each point**
- Tell me everything in the report

> A good summary starts with who it's for and what they need, and gives page numbers so each point can be checked.

**2. An AI summary says a contract can be cancelled with 30 days' notice. You'll act on it in an hour. What do you do?** _([Lecture 13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=blIzU7NFEMg))_

- Trust it: summaries are reliable
- **Find that clause in the original, and read it yourself**
- Ask the AI to summarise it again
- Ask a second AI to confirm

> A summary helps you find things fast. For anything you'll act on, the source has the final say.

**3. A summary of a long report says the project covers all four sites. What kind of mistake should you check for?** _([Lecture 13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=blIzU7NFEMg))_

- Spelling mistakes
- Nothing: numbers of sites are always right
- The font of the report
- **A dropped exception, such as one site that is excluded**

> Summaries often drop exceptions and conditions, which are exactly what a decision-maker needs. Check them in the source.

**4. AI rewrites your complaint reply to sound more caring, and the new version offers a full refund. Is it ready to send?** _([Lecture 14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tdOqNPgBhRU))_

- **No. It added a promise you never made**
- Yes, it sounds more caring
- Yes, if it's under 100 words
- Only if the customer asked for a refund

> A rewrite can change the meaning, not just the tone. Always check facts, tone and promises before sending.

**5. What should you ask first when you add a spreadsheet to an AI chat?** _([Lecture 15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YqFNTDclLQM))_

- Write a summary for my manager
- **Describe this data: how many rows, which columns, and any missing or odd values**
- Make a chart
- Why did sales drop?

> A health check catches problems early, such as two date formats, spelling differences or a test row.

**6. AI says your total revenue for the year is ₹86 lakh. What's the quickest way to trust that number?** _([Lecture 15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YqFNTDclLQM))_

- Ask the AI if it's sure
- Ask a colleague if it sounds right
- Round it to ₹90 lakh
- **Add up the revenue column yourself in the spreadsheet, and compare**

> Check one key number yourself. If it doesn't match, ask the AI exactly how it calculated it.

**7. Your sales data has columns for date, outlet, product, units and price. AI says one outlet dropped in March because of road works nearby. What is that?** _([Lecture 15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YqFNTDclLQM))_

- **A guess, because nothing in the data shows road works**
- A finding, because AI calculated it
- A fact, because it sounds specific
- A chart

> Data shows what happened. If the cause isn't in the data, the explanation is a guess, not a finding.

**8. You photograph a printed price list and ask AI to turn it into a table. It looks perfect. What's next?** _([Lecture 16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0ATl-rG33QU))_

- Send it to the customer straight away
- Ask the AI to make it colourful
- **Compare the prices with the printed list first**
- Delete the photo

> AI can misread small text and digits. One misread digit, and you've quoted the wrong price.

**9. AI quotes a market figure and links to a real news site that loads fine. Are you done?** _([Lecture 17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TfI7EOc6rD8))_

- Yes: the link works
- **Not yet. Find that figure on the page, and check its date**
- Yes, if the site is well known
- Only if the number is round

> A real page is not the same as a real claim. The figure may be from another year, or not there at all.

**10. Which source is best for an important number in your report?** _([Lecture 17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TfI7EOc6rD8))_

- A blog that quotes another blog
- The AI's answer from memory
- **A primary source, such as the government report or the company's own filing**
- A social media post

> Ask for primary sources, open them, and find the exact number. If you haven't read it, don't cite it.
